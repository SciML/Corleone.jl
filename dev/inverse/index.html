Parameter estimation

CorleoneInverse fits the tunable values of a Corleone ShootingLayer to measurements. You describe the data as an Experiment, declare which parameters and initial states to estimate with the usual Tunable and Constant annotations, and solve with NonlinearSolve.jl or Optimization.jl. A fit can then report covariances and confidence intervals.

A first fit

We estimate the decay rate of $\dot u = -p u$ from ten noise-free samples of $e^{-1.3 t}$, starting from the guess $p = 0.5$:

using CorleoneInverse, OrdinaryDiffEqTsit5, NonlinearSolve

ode = ODEProblem((u, p, t) -> -p[1] .* u, [1.0], (0.0, 2.0), [0.5])
shooting = ShootingLayer(ode, Tsit5(), Tunable(1) => nothing)
t = collect(0.2:0.2:2.0)
experiment = Experiment(1 => (t, exp.(-1.3 .* t), 0.01))
model = CorleoneDynamicOptProblem(shooting, experiment)
fit = solve(NonlinearLeastSquaresProblem(model), LevenbergMarquardt())
ParameterEstimate(model, fit.u)
ParameterEstimate (RSS = 4.559e-8, dof = 9)
  index  value  std. error  95.0% interval
  1  1.3  0.0139  [1.2727, 1.3272]

Experiment(1 => (t, y, σ)) says that state 1 was measured at times t with values y and noise standard deviation σ. CorleoneDynamicOptProblem sets up ps and st itself; pass both explicitly to reuse an existing Lux setup.

Residuals and objectives

For every observed value the model forms the noise-scaled residual

\[r = \frac{y - h(u(t), p, t)}{\sigma},\]

where $h$ is the observed quantity. Give a noise scale for every observation or for none; without scales, residuals are unweighted.

Two solver problems are available:

Solver problemUse it for
NonlinearLeastSquaresProblem(model)Plain least squares with annotation bounds and untransformed Normal priors.
OptimizationProblem(model, ad)General priors, user constraints, and multiple-shooting equalities.

The scalar objective used by OptimizationProblem is the mean squared residual minus log_prior. Pass reducer = x -> sum(x) / 2 to the model constructor for the Gaussian half-sum, which is the scaling the statistics assume when priors are present. The reducer receives the squared, noise-scaled residuals; priors and constraints are added separately.

Uncertainty estimates

ParameterEstimate(model, sol.u) evaluates the observation Jacobian $J$ at the fit and returns the covariance

\[\left(J^\top J / s^2 + H\right)^{-1},\]

where $H$ is the Hessian of the negative log prior. Equality constraints, including multiple-shooting continuity, are respected by restricting the covariance to their null space.

  • With known noise scales, $s^2 = 1$ and intervals use normal quantiles.
  • Without them, $s^2$ is the residual variance and intervals use Student-t quantiles.
  • Results are reported in natural coordinates: the covariance through the delta method, and confidence intervals by mapping their endpoints from internal coordinates.
Assumptions

The estimate is a local, linearized approximation. It assumes an interior optimum, away from active inequalities and parameter bounds. With priors, it also assumes known noise scales and a fit of half the weighted residual sum of squares minus the log prior: use least squares with Normal priors, or reducer = x -> sum(x) / 2 with OptimizationProblem.
